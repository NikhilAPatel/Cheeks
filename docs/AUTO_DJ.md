# Auto DJ

One button: with two tracks loaded, the app performs a creative transition from the playing deck to the other. The bar is a touring house DJ, not an automix. This is the design and the evidence behind it. The lab that tests it lives in `tools/autodj/`.

## The approach

**Plan the transition and play it through the real controls. Don't generate audio.** The app already has the instruments: stems, loops, FX, EQ, key sync. What's missing is something that decides what to do and performs it on the beat.

1. **Track map.** For every bar: which stems are playing, whether there's a kick, whether someone's singing, how big it is, and what notes are sounding. Also downbeats and phrase lines.
2. **Moves.** Each is a beat-locked score of control changes. They follow fixed rules: one bassline at a time, handed over on a downbeat; one kick at a time; two vocals never overlap; harmony overlaps only if the two parts agree. Moves that overlap nothing work between any two tracks.
3. **Planner.** Tries each move at every exit and entry point, throws out what breaks the rules, and ranks the rest.
4. **Audition.** Renders the best few options faster than real time and rejects let-downs: a drop quieter than what came before it, a hole, a bump, a pushed limiter. This runs before anything is heard.
5. **Performer.** Plays the winner. It moves the same controls a person would, so the knobs visibly turn and any of them can be taken back by touching it.
6. **Taste.** Ratings tune the ranking.

## What the research says

## Measured so far

On 16 Creative Commons dance tracks from [MTG-Jamendo](https://mtg.github.io/mtg-jamendo-dataset/):

- **Grids:** 12 of 16 tracks have kicks within 13.5 ms of a straight grid, and most tempos land on whole numbers. The other four are flagged as not steady, and only get moves that don't overlap anything.
- **Downbeats:** the lab's phase agrees with [Beat This](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this) (a separately trained model, MIT licence) on 15 of 16 tracks. The one disagreement is a track the steadiness check already flags. Neither is ground truth, but they get there independently.
- **The app's old grid:** on the same tracks it had the wrong 1 on 8 of 16 and sat about 100 ms late on 4. It gave one steady track a false tempo map too. Stems now correct it.
- **Kick attacks against the corrected grid:** 5.2 ms median on the 12 steady tracks, with 2.0 ms of spread between tracks. The old grid's spread was 44 ms.
- **Swift against Python:** same tempo on all 12 steady tracks, same 1 on 11, and near-identical stem maps. `tools/autodj/compare_map.py` checks it. The one that differs (jam225361) is a low-confidence downbeat.
- **Phrase lines:** 32% of [CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06823)'s cue points on the 12 steady tracks fall on our 16-bar lines (chance is 6%), and 44% on 8-bar lines (chance is 12%). A sharper detector scored lower, so it was dropped. CUE-DETR is a development check and doesn't ship: it's a heavy model, and it needs a lot of images per track.
- **Lock:** the incoming deck ends each move within a fraction of a microsecond of where the clock says.
- **First kick of a drop on a stretched deck:** was about 60 dB down. It's now within about 1 dB of the kicks after it on three of four test tracks, and 6 dB louder on the fourth.
- **The audition earns its place:** across six pairs it rejected holes, a bump, a pushed limiter and drops landing low.
- **Speed on this M1 Pro:** a track map takes about 2 s, a plan 10 ms, and a 60 s audition under a second. Nothing has been measured on a phone.

**Not measured: whether any of it sounds good.** The [Listening Room](https://claude.ai/artifact/25J24fKfhoHEMu5QdouQzW) collects blind ratings against a plain crossfade, on what the app's own code renders. That's the real test.

## What the owner has said by ear

On their own tracks, from the local Listening Room (`tools/autodj/local_room.py`):

- **Loop build: Wow.** The one beat of air before the drop created suspense. It now scores higher.
- **Vocal ride: Good.** The handover between the two vocals worked, and it may suit other songs better.
- **Vocal tease: Good.** The new vocal came in well, but the new track's arrival was better on the loop build.
- **Bass swap: OK.** Pleasant, but plain, close to a normal crossfade. So it now has a moment in it: the old track rises and loses its low end for its last bar, everything stops for one beat, and the new track lands whole. Its air measures about −30 dB, the same as the loop build's, and 12 to 14 dB below its landing. This version hasn't been rated yet. Its takes end `__r2`, and `tools/autodj/check_swap.py` measures them.
- **Brake drop: Bad.** Way too jarring. It's now only used between tracks nothing else can join.
- **Crossfade, 125 into 126 BPM:** the second song felt far too slow. This was the room's plain control, which isn't something AUTO plays, and it faded a full section into bar 0 of a song with no drums for its first 72 seconds. The control now has the new track's full band in by the end of the fade. Its new takes end `__r2`, and the old rating stays under `__r1`.

That's six takes, mostly from one pair, so these are early signs.

**How fast a track feels.** `tools/autodj/feel.py` counts what each stem plays on a 16th-note grid in a track's main sections, and how much it swings. Tracks at nearly the same tempo can differ a lot: in their main sections, Turn Off The Lights (125 BPM) has 0.93 bass hits a beat and Sweet Release (126 BPM) has 3.97. The planner doesn't use this yet, because no rating shows the difference matters. The complaint above came from the missing drums, and every AUTO take rendered so far lands with kick and bass on in at least 7 of the first 8 bars. The unrated takes of that pair will show whether it does.

## Mixing through a list

`MIX` in the browser takes the list on screen, in the order it's showing. It carries on from a track that's already playing, or starts from the top. It differs from a press of `AUTO` in three ways:

- **It gives each track its time.** Someone pressing `AUTO` wants it soon, but a mix starts looking to leave once a track's last big section is under way, or 60% of the way through one that never lets up (`TransitionPlanner.setExit`). If no plan passes from there, it looks 16 bars further back each time, but no further than a third of the way in.
- **It keeps the moves varied.** The last four moves count against playing them again. It leaves out the brake drop, which the owner found way too jarring, as nobody's there to take it back.
- **Someone taking over ends it.** Loading another track on the waiting deck, starting it, or stopping the playing deck all do. Moving the playing deck just makes it plan again.

If there's no clean way in, or stems aren't ready in time, the next track starts when the last one ends, so the music doesn't stop.

**Measured with `tools/autodj/app_set/`, which runs a set through the app's own planner and audition without playing it.** On the owner's 22 tracks, in folder order:

- 19 of 21 joins found a move, and 2 fell back to starting the next track at the end.
- Moves: loop build 7, echo throw 7, bass swap 3, vocal tease 1, vocal ride 1.
- A track typically played 50 to 90% before the move. The shortest was 35%, where nothing passed from later on.

Folder order is a harsh test, as it's in no musical order. Whether it holds together for an hour is a question for ears. Nothing has been measured on a phone either: a split has to finish while the track before it plays, and how long a split takes there is unknown.

## What to train, and what not to

**Not worth training:**
- Fader or EQ curves. The evidence above says they aren't the problem.
- An end-to-end audio generator. It couldn't be interrupted, would damage sound quality, and needs far more than a phone has.
- Beats and downbeats. Stems already do this well.

**Worth training: a stem compatibility model.** It answers "will this vocal sit on that instrumental?", which is the question behind the most creative moves. Today a hand-made table of musical intervals answers it, and that's the weakest thing in the planner.
- **Data:** MTG-Jamendo has 2,169 house, 2,179 techno and 16,480 electronic tracks, all Creative Commons, all full length. No human labels are needed, and no copyrighted DJ mixes.
- **Compute:** about 5,000 tracks of stems at about 30× real time is about 13 GPU hours, plus training. One recent 24 GB GPU and 2 TB of fast disk is enough. This Mac has 18 GB free, so it can only prove the pipeline.
- **On the phone:** a small network over bar-length mel spectrograms of the stems, run on `.cpuAndGPU`.
- **Bar to ship it:** it has to beat the interval table on rated pairs. If it doesn't, it doesn't ship.

**Later, only if ratings plateau:** a critic trained on real DJ transitions. The best data is commercial mixes on YouTube, which means copyright and terms-of-service problems, so I won't collect it. Internet Archive has some Creative Commons DJ mixes of Creative Commons tracks, which is a cleaner and smaller option.

**Licences:** MTG-Jamendo tracks carry individual Creative Commons licences, some non-commercial. For a personal app that's fine. If Cheeks is ever sold, retrain on the commercial-friendly subset.

## Limits

- **Top-level DJs prepare.** Festival transitions are often studio edits and rehearsed mashups. Live automation can sound great without being that.
- **Test music:** these tracks aren't club hits. Quality has to be judged on music the owner actually plays.
- **Phone speed:** stem splitting ran about 12× real time on this Mac's GPU, and nothing has been measured on a phone. The best moves need both tracks split.

## In the app

- **`AUTO`** on the mixer. It needs one deck playing, the other loaded and stopped, and stems on both. It waits for stems that are still splitting.
- **Press:** plans, auditions, then waits for the bar and counts down. Press again to call it off.
- **Mid-move:** the knobs and stem levels follow the score, and touching one takes it back. Pressing `AUTO` stops the score and leaves the decks as they are.
- **At the end:** the old deck's stopped and both decks are back to neutral. The new track carries on at the playing tempo.
